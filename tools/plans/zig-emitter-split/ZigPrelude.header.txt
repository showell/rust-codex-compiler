 THE ZIG RUNTIME, AS SOURCE. Every definition here is a fragment of the zig
 program the emitter writes above its own output: the allocator, the linked
 lists, the text and CCE routines, the deck, and the entry point's plumbing.
 None of it decides anything. `zig-prelude-parts` is the table naming the
 fragments, and the emitter shakes that table against a program to keep only
 the parts that program reaches.

 IT IS THE SAME CHAPTER AS `ZigEmitter.codex`, split the way `CSharpEmitter`
 and `CSharpEmitterExpressions` are split -- one chapter, two files. Nothing
 needs a cite it did not already have, and the emitter reads these names
 exactly as it did when they sat below it.

 WHY IT IS ITS OWN FILE. This is zig, and everything else in the chapter is
 Codex reasoning about IR. A reader who knows zig can correct a routine here
 without meeting the emitter at all, and a reader working on the emitter never
 has to scroll through four hundred and seventy-five lines of someone else's
 language. The two halves also change for different reasons: a fragment moves
 when zig or the runtime contract moves, and the emitter moves when the IR does.

 A FRAGMENT LIST IS NOT A STRING. `ShakeLit` is literal text and `ShakeUse`
 names another part, so the shaker can follow one fragment to the next and drop
 what nothing reaches. Writing a part's name inside a `ShakeLit` makes it
 invisible to that walk, and the part it needed is then shaken out of a program
 that calls it.
